## dry-125: what went wrong

1. After Stockline's price rose to 0.04 (over the Sourcing agent's 0.03 cap), the Sourcing agent stopped buying
   (cache only) instead of trying and being refused. Code only let Treasury design a rebalance after "a purchase
   refused over a cap", which never came. Treasury even asked Sourcing to try a 0.04 buy so it would be refused; both
   agents then agreed that would be staging a breach and withdrew it (good). Result: the shop turned every customer
   away for 12 minutes, only 5 stock checks were sold, and Summit's dispute (#5 to #16) was about sales that mostly
   never happened.
2. The dispute script then crashed: of the disputed sales only #5 existed, none had wrong data, and routing an empty
   refund batch is an error by design. The customer's "refund all" never reached the CFO.

Fix (with tests; a fresh Opus review found nothing serious, and its points are in): code's evidence that an agent is blocked by its mandate now also counts
customers turned away (at least 3 in 15 minutes) while no supplier sells fresh data (at most 5 minutes old) within
the cap; the words for the CFO never say a purchase was refused when none was. A dispute where no delivered sale is
wrong now refunds nothing and still sends the customer's request to the CFO.

After the review: the CFO's evidence for the new case is the turned-away ledger rows themselves (type `turned_away`,
their id range and a hash of the rows as stored), never a "refusal" that did not happen; the newer of the two kinds of
evidence wins; a mandate that cannot be read proves nothing; code's cheap checks run before the chain is read. Not
covered by a test: the dispute script's empty-refund path end to end (only its plan, `test/refund-plan.test.ts`); the
next dry runs exercise it only if the world repeats dry-125.

## dry-131 and dry-132, on the fixed code

- dry-132: green (24 checks, 0 fallbacks of 21 decisions, 87 movements with their origin).
- dry-131: red. The agents stalled again after the price rise, and this time the new evidence worked: Treasury saw
  "41 customer queries turned away" and proposed the rebalance (P-0002/P-0003). But it saw it only at its next tick,
  about 8 minutes after the price rise; the dry run had stopped waiting for options after 7 minutes, so nobody
  approved them and the shop stayed shut. Then `npm run verify` failed on the rebalance's credentials: they are
  signed when proposed and bound only after approval, and verify demanded every credential be bound.
- Fixes (tests in `test/buy-policy.test.ts`, `test/verify-unexecuted-credential.test.ts`): the third customer turned
  away in 15 minutes wakes Treasury at once (it still reads code's evidence, not the event); verify checks a
  credential of a proposal that was not executed (pending, rejected, expired) for the opposite: it must not be bound,
  and fails if it is. An executed proposal's credential gets the full check as before. Re-run on dry-131: verify
  passes, both P-0002 credentials "issued for P-0002 (PENDING_APPROVAL), never bound".
- Second review of these two fixes: nothing weakened verify, but a real regression: options A and B of a price rise
  share credentials (named after A), so a CFO choosing B would bind a credential whose file names a superseded
  proposal, and verify would have failed it. Fixed: the decision goes by the credential's hash over every proposal
  that names it; bound is fine once one of them was approved (a crashed or partial replace included), unbound is fine
  while none was executed; a chain read that fails is a failed line, not an aborted verify. The wake now fires at most
  once per 15 minutes (it would have woken Treasury on every refusal when they came minutes apart).
