# Hands-on demo load test, local only (run 12 November 2026)

**Result:** on a local `wrangler dev` (one workerd process on the build container, not Cloudflare), 50 of 50 visitors started a test company, filed the clean sample and polled the engine's events every second for 20 seconds. No answer was a 5xx or a 429, and the Claude spend stayed at 0 micro-dollars. On the kit11 Worker's `/try/sign`, 50 visitors each made a test world and asked the chain, the trick and "talk the agent round" with no 5xx. One address asking for 8 worlds got 6, then 429 twice: the hourly limit held.

**Local, not deployed.** These figures come from one local process on a shared build container, with every visitor starting in the same second. They show that the limits and the budget hold and that nothing fails. They do not show the deployed Workers' latency. A run against purser-try or kit11 needs the owner's word through the orchestrator.

|---|---|

| Claude | Not called. purser-try ran with `ENGINE_CLAUDE=off` and no key, so Purser's code read every invoice. The kit11 Worker had no model key, so `/talk` answered with the labelled fallback. No live calls of any kind. |

## The bot check's solve time

Measured in the pre-installed Chromium on the build container, with the browser's own solver (`BOT_SOLVER_JS`) at 16 bits, 20 challenges each. With the CPU unthrottled: median 34 ms, p90 60 ms, max 83 ms. With the CPU slowed 4 times (DevTools `Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate`, used here as a stand-in for a mid-range phone): median 92 ms, p90 238 ms, max 276 ms. Under 1 s at normal load. At 20 bits, the most under heavy load, the work is 16 times more.
