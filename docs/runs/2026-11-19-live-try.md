# The public /try, walked 8 times as a visitor, 19 November 2026

The judge's journey on the public demo, https://kit11.max-31b.workers.dev/try, run end to end 8 times in Chromium with a
virtual passkey, with every answer the page got kept: 4 runs at 1440 px in light mode, 4 at 390 px (a phone) in dark mode.
Each run is a fresh /try test company. **Test money only**: the company's bank is the simulated bank, and its payouts go
to the **Airwallex sandbox**, where PAID is reached through the sandbox's own simulation endpoint, not by any real
settlement. Nothing was deployed, no admin route was called and no key of ours was used: the walks are what any visitor's
browser does.

**Result: 8 of 8 runs passed every step.** 16 Airwallex sandbox transfers, each SCHEDULED then PAID; 8 approvals whose
hash is the one the 1,850.00 transfer carries; 8 one-cent changes refused with no transfer; 8 EUR 480.00 invoices held by
code with no payout; 16 proof files, each VALID on /verify and with `npm run verify-bundle`. 120 Claude entries (185
requests to the API) cost USD 0.0392, all by `claude-haiku-5-5`, none fell back to the parser. Two things look wrong
and are said below: the reader's Claude calls carry no request id in the engine's events, and two runs started less
than 30 s after the one before.

## What each run did

Recorded by [`scripts/try-live-record.ts`](../../scripts/try-live-record.ts) (`npx tsx scripts/try-live-record.ts <run>
<desktop|phone> docs/runs/2026-11-19-live-try`), the walk of `test/browser/judge-try.check.ts --live` with the answers
kept and the steps timed:

1. **Launch**: "Launch the live transfer demo" makes a test company (`POST /v1/try/start`, 201).
2. **EUR 120.00, paid by the agent alone**: Claude reads the invoice, the checker, coder and payer agents run, the
   policy approves it inside the agent's EUR 500.00 limit, "See the agent pay it" pays it (proposal EXECUTED) and the
   page asks the Airwallex sandbox to simulate the transfer to PAID.
3. **EUR 1,850.00, approved with a passkey, then paid**: sent to the CFO; the page makes a passkey on Chromium's virtual
   authenticator (CDP `WebAuthn.addVirtualAuthenticator`: ctap2, internal, `hasUserVerification` and `isUserVerified`
   true; not a person's Face ID) and signs the exact terms with it; the signed pay is verified and paid; the transfer is
   simulated to PAID.
4. **The one-cent change**: the page's tamper test sends 1,850.01 with the earlier signature: refused.
5. **The proof**: "Download the proof (JSON)" on the result screen, then that file picked on
   https://kit11.max-31b.workers.dev/verify; the paid proof (from the pay answer) pasted there too. Both also checked
   with the repository's verifier.
6. **EUR 480.00, held**: in a second tab (a test company of its own, as the judge check does), the sample "Inside the
   limit — but cash says wait": read and checked by the agents, held by code's cash check, no payout.

Every run also checked that no request left the site's host and that the page logged no console or page error: 0 and 0
in all 8.

## What failed or looked wrong

- **The reader's Claude calls have no request id.** In all 8 runs the engine's `claude.read` events (read, classify,
  review: 56 calls) carry `request_id: null`; only the agents' turns carry one (129 of 185 requests). The result screen
  says "Each call is logged with its model, time, cost and Anthropic's request id": for the reader's calls on the
  deployed site, it is not. The reader takes the id from the SDK's answer (`src/claude/reader.ts`, `_request_id`), which
  came back empty here. Found and fixed after this record: SDK 1.3.0's `beta.messages.parse()` returns a copy of
  the message that drops the non-enumerable `_request_id`; the reader now reads the answer's `request-id` header itself
  (`test/engine-budget.test.ts`). Not on the deployed site until the next deploy; these 8 runs stay as recorded.
- **Two runs started less than 30 s after the one before.** Run 2 started 24 s after run 1 ended, and attempt 3a (below)
  9 s after run 2 ended. Every other gap was 36 to 56 s; start to start, no two walks were less than 74 s apart.
- **One attempt stopped by the recording script, not by the site** (`attempt-3a.json`, 07:01:40 to 07:03:24 UTC, 1440
  light). For the 1,850.00 invoice the page's own passkey button ("Approve & sign EUR 1,850.00 with your passkey")
  signed straight away, while the script waited 60 s for a separate "Approve & sign" sheet. The site's steps had all
  completed: EUR 120.00 paid alone, Airwallex `37279400-3f18-458f-87c6-21ec84e671d5` SCHEDULED → PAID; EUR 1,850.00
  approved and paid (approval_sha256 `30c3342e024d58f232e02425d49d7e53ffcc9e3b54a8d38a7711f14d6254df30`), Airwallex
  `83f6758a-e3f3-4c9f-9199-643ce652c89d` SCHEDULED → PAID. Its refusal, proof, hold and events were not reached or not
  read. The script then took the click that leads to the approval, whichever button it is, and runs 3 to 8 ran with it.
  It is not counted among the 8 runs.
- **Run 1 has no "Pay" time.** The script read the request's timing before the answer had finished (the same bug made
  the per-call times of run 1's list empty); fixed before run 2. The Pay row above has n = 7.
- **An exploratory walk before the record** (06:54:50 to 06:55:44 UTC, 1440 light, not kept here: its capture held the
  session credential): the same journey, all steps passed; Airwallex `63c77f21-773b-49ad-aa98-63b66bfa866d` (EUR
  120.00) and `319913fc-f668-4eec-bd77-3d3e26fee65f` (EUR 1,850.00), both SCHEDULED → PAID; 11 Claude entries, USD
  0.0044. With attempt 3a, today's walks made 20 Airwallex sandbox transfers in all, all PAID; 18 test companies.
- **Looked odd, explained by the code.** `POST /v1/try/start` answers `mode.rail_awx: "off"` and each pay answer says
  `sandbox_calls: 0`, while the payouts did go to the Airwallex sandbox. Those two name another leg (the switch
  `rail_awx`); the payout is the separate `awx_payout` leg, whose answer is the `airwallex_payout` note with
  `source: "airwallex-sandbox"` (`cloud/src/engine/livePayout.ts`).
- **What the events do not hold.** The engine's events have no row for the refusal (only the visitor's `try.pay`) or for
  the hold: the refusal is in the pay answer and the downloaded proof, the hold in the sample answer's `cash` check. The
  refused pay's executor layer gives the proposal's state ("P-0002 is EXECUTED, not approved") as its reason; the
  signature layer is the one that names the changed terms.
