# The public /try, a mega run: 17 walks one after another, 19 November 2026

The judge's journey on the public demo, https://kit11.max-31b.workers.dev/try, run end to end again and again as a
visitor, one walk at a time, with every answer the page got kept: in Chromium at 1440 px light, 390 px dark (a phone)
and 768 px (a tablet, light and dark), and in Firefox at 1440 light and 390 dark. The same walk as this morning's 8
([2026-11-19-live-try.md](2026-11-19-live-try.md)). **Test money only**: each walk is a fresh /try test company on the
simulated bank, its payouts go to the **Airwallex sandbox**, where PAID is reached through the sandbox's own simulation
endpoint. Nothing was deployed, no admin route was called and no key of ours was used.

**50 walks were planned; 17 ran.** The series stopped itself after walk 17 on its own rule (3 walks in a row marked
failed), and it would have had to stop one walk later anyway: the deployed /try makes at most **100 Airwallex sandbox
payouts a UTC day for all its visitors** (`AWX_PAYOUT_DAY` in `worker/src/awxPayout.ts`, 2 per walk), and our own walks
had used 58 of them by then. We agreed to leave at least 40 of the day's payouts to other visitors and judges, so the
remaining 33 walks are left for another UTC day (the series resumes where it stopped; see [Resuming](#resuming)).

**Result: every step the site was asked to do passed in 16 of 17 walks; the recording script marked 13 of 17 "all
ok".** The 4 it did not: walk 2 was stopped by a bug in the script itself, after the proof download; walks 15, 16 and
17 passed every site step, and were marked failed only because /verify, redeployed during the series, now says "Proof
valid · payment refused" for a refusal's valid proof, words the script did not know yet. 34 Airwallex sandbox
transfers, each SCHEDULED then PAID; 17 approvals whose hash is the one the 1,850.00 transfer carries; 17 one-cent
changes refused with no transfer; 16 EUR 480.00 invoices held by code with no payout (walk 2 did not reach it); 34 of 34
proof files VALID with `npm run verify-bundle`, 32 of them also VALID on /verify (walk 2's were not opened there). 240
Claude entries (371 requests to the API, 259 distinct request ids) cost USD 0.078697, all by `claude-haiku-5-5`, **none
fell back to the parser**. No 5xx; one 429, from the script's own reading of the engine's events, answered by a 60 s
back-off.

## What each walk did

The walk of [2026-11-19-live-try.md](2026-11-19-live-try.md#what-each-run-did), unchanged: launch; EUR 120.00 read,
checked and paid by the agent alone, simulated to PAID in the Airwallex sandbox; EUR 1,850.00 sent to the CFO, a passkey
made and the exact terms signed, paid, simulated to PAID; the one-cent change (1,850.01) with the earlier signature,
refused; the proof downloaded and checked on /verify (and the paid proof pasted there), both also with the repository's
verifier; in a second tab, a test company of its own, the EUR 480.00 invoice that code's cash check holds. Every walk
also checked that no request left the site's host and that the page logged no console or page error.

Recorded by [`scripts/try-live-record.ts`](../../scripts/try-live-record.ts), one process per walk, driven by
[`scripts/try-live-series.ts`](../../scripts/try-live-series.ts):

- **One at a time.** A walk starts only when the one before has ended and at least 35 s have passed (and at least 30 s
  after the previous start). Measured: 35.0 to 95.1 s from one walk's end to the next one's start, 90.1 to 160.8 s start
  to start.
- **Backing off.** After a walk that saw a 429 or a 5xx from the site, the next waits 60 s more (120 s if it happens
  again in a row). It happened once: walk 1's 429, so walk 2 waited 95 s.
- **Stopping.** After 3 walks in a row that failed or in which Claude fell back, or on a full disk.
- **The passkey.** Chromium: its virtual authenticator over CDP (ctap2, internal, user verification on and verified),
  as this morning. Firefox has no virtual authenticator under Playwright, so the repository's software passkey
  ([`test/browser/support/softPasskey.ts`](../../test/browser/support/softPasskey.ts), test only) is put in every page
  before it loads: a real ES256 key made with WebCrypto, whose registration and signatures the server checks as it does
  a phone's. It needed one addition to sign on /try: its credentials now answer `getClientExtensionResults()` (with
  `{}`), which /try reads for every passkey. Not a person's Face ID in either browser.
- **Firefox's trust store.** Outbound HTTPS from this machine goes through a proxy with its own certificate authority,
  which Chromium trusts from the machine's NSS store (`~/.pki/nssdb`) and Firefox does not (its profile has its own).
  With `NSSDB=<dir>` the script starts Firefox on a fresh profile whose certificate store is a copy of that NSS store.
  No certificate check is turned off.
- **Planned order** (50): walks 5, 13, 21, 29, 37 and 45 at 768 (light, then dark, alternating); 9, 17, 25, 33, 41 and
  49 in Firefox (1440 light, then 390 dark, alternating); the rest alternating Chromium 1440 light and 390 dark. Of the
  17 that ran: Chromium 1440 light 7, Chromium 390 dark 6, Chromium 768 light 1 (walk 5), Chromium 768 dark 1 (walk 13),
  Firefox 1440 light 1 (walk 9), Firefox 390 dark 1 (walk 17).
