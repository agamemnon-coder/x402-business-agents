# The public /try on an iPhone, walked 9 times as a judge: WebKit, Chromium, and Chromium on Slow 4G (21 November 2026)

The judge's journey on the public demo, https://kit11.max-31b.workers.dev/try, run end to end **as an iPhone 15**
(Playwright's own device: 393 x 659, its Safari user agent, touch, 3x, light), 9 times in turn: **in WebKit** (the engine
of Safari), **in Chromium**, and **in Chromium on a throttled network** (DevTools' "Slow 4G": 562.5 ms added round
trip, 1.6 Mbit/s down, 750 kbit/s up). Each walk is a fresh /try test company. **Test money only**: the company's bank
is the simulated bank, and its payouts go to the **Airwallex sandbox**, where PAID is reached through the sandbox's own
simulation endpoint, not by any real settlement. Nothing was deployed, no admin route was called and no key of ours was
used: the walks are what any visitor's browser does. Recorded before judging, which starts Monday 23 November.

**Result: 8 of 9 walks passed every step; all 3 in WebKit and all 3 on Slow 4G did.** 16 Airwallex sandbox
transfers, each SCHEDULED then PAID (2.8 s p50 from one to the other); 8 approvals whose hash is the one the EUR 1,850.00
transfer carries; 8 one-cent changes refused with no transfer; 8 EUR 480.00 invoices held by code with no payout; 16
proof files, each valid on /verify and with the repository's verifier. 120 Claude entries (188 requests to the API) cost
USD 0.041367 at list price, all by `claude-haiku-5-5`, **none fell back to the parser**. 663 calls to `/v1`: 646
answered 200 and 17 answered 201; **no 429, no 5xx**. 0 console errors, 0 requests to another host.

**Walk 2 (Chromium) stopped at the first payment**: the EUR 120.00 invoice, inside the agent's limit, went to the CFO
for a passkey instead of being paid by the agent alone (the pay answer: `PENDING_APPROVAL`). The page showed the
approval screen for EUR 120.00 ("You are the CFO. Approve this exact payment."), and the recorder, waiting for the
sandbox transfer of a payment made alone, stopped there after 120 s. Code's own checks on that invoice said pay (the
kept sample answer: cash `outcome: "pay"`, `decided_by: "code"`, within the EUR 500.00 limit); Purser pays alone only
when the Checker agent decided pay and the Payer agent's proposal, enforced by code, is on the engine's own plan
(`src/ap/pay.ts` `payAlone`, `src/ap/payerAgent.ts` `payerGate`), and anything short of that leaves the payment to the
CFO. Which of the two agents said otherwise this record cannot tell: the recorder read the engine's events only after
the proof, so a walk stopped earlier kept none. It reads them on a stop now (`scripts/try-live-record.ts`, the change
of this day). The walk is recorded as it ran and counted as failed.

## How each walk ran

Recorded by [`scripts/try-live-series.ts`](../../scripts/try-live-series.ts) with `PLAN=phones` (one walk at a time, at
least 35 s apart; walk n takes WebKit, Chromium, then Chromium on Slow 4G, in turn), each walk being
[`scripts/try-live-record.ts`](../../scripts/try-live-record.ts) with the profile `phone-iphone`; then summed by
[`scripts/try-live-summary.ts`](../../scripts/try-live-summary.ts), which wrote `summary.json`:

    PLAN=phones npx tsx scripts/try-live-series.ts docs/runs/2026-11-21-phone-try <log dir> 1 9
    npx tsx scripts/try-live-summary.ts docs/runs/2026-11-21-phone-try

The walk is the one of the earlier records (docs/runs/2026-11-19-live-try.md): launch; the EUR 120.00 invoice read
and paid by the agent alone; the EUR 1,850.00 invoice read, sent to the CFO, a passkey made and the exact terms signed,
then paid; the one-cent change refused; the proof downloaded and checked on /verify and with the repository's verifier;
and in a second tab, the EUR 480.00 invoice that code's cash check holds. In WebKit, Playwright has no virtual
authenticator, so the walk uses the repository's software passkey
([`test/browser/support/softPasskey.ts`](../../test/browser/support/softPasskey.ts), TEST ONLY: a real ES256 key in the
page, whose signatures the server verifies as it does a phone's); it proves nothing about Face ID. The page, seeing an
iPhone, named the button "Approve & sign EUR 1,850.00 with Face ID" in every walk that got there; no Face ID was used.
The throttle is set on every page of the walk before it loads (DevTools Protocol, `Network.emulateNetworkConditions`)
and is written in each record as `network`; every `/v1` answer in those walks took at least 613 ms (323 ms unthrottled).
