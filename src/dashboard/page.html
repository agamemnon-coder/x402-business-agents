How the CFO signs in

There is no password and no account to break into. Two things open Purser, and both stay on the CFO's own devices.

1. A private link to look

The runner on the company's own machine prints a viewer link. Its key sits after the #, so the browser keeps it and never sends it to a server. Open it once on the laptop or the phone. A new relay secret gives a new link and locks out every old one.

2. Face ID to decide

Approving needs the CFO's passkey: Face ID or Touch ID on the phone, bound to this site. It signs the exact terms (amount, payee, fee), and the runner checks that signature against the key it pinned when the CFO enrolled. A copied link can look, but never approve.

3. Enrolment you confirm

A new passkey only counts after the CFO reads its fingerprint off the phone and confirms it on the runner. A second approver for large amounts is added the same way.

You are a guest, so there is nothing to sign in to here. Everything the CFO sees is in the recorded CFO dashboard, and the demo walks through one real run step by step. To run it yourself, the README has the steps.

For CFOs of companies that run AI agents

Your AI agents spend through Airwallex and Metal. The CFO stays in control.

Each agent gets its own Airwallex company card and its own Metal mandate. You set the limits once; Airwallex and the chain enforce them. Anything more waits for your Face ID.

Why Airwallex customers need this: Northwind Outdoor, an outdoor wholesaler that sells live stock data to retailers per query, buys software, pays suppliers abroad and sells across currencies. As AI agents take over that work, each needs its own card and limits, and the CFO one place for the exceptions.

Where the money moves, and where the limits hold

Purser is the control layer. The money moves on Airwallex and Metal, and they enforce the limits: a card declines at Airwallex, a payment is refused by Metal against the on-chain mandate. Everything below happened in the recorded run, except the block marked as separate runs and the Dispute desk case, run afterwards on a copy of its ledger.

      Pay per request on Metal

      For payments of a few cents each, a limit the chain enforces.

      Agents that buy data or API calls pay a few cents per request on Metal. Each pays under a mandate: a cap per payment, a total, one allowed payee and an end date. Ask for more than the mandate allows and Metal refuses against the on-chain mandate; the CFO decides with the same Face ID.

      Safe by design, not by prompt

      Nothing an AI reads or writes can raise a limit or move money outside it. The limits sit at Airwallex and on the chain; exceptions need you.

      Face ID on the exact terms

      You sign the precise amount, payee and fee. Change one character and the signature no longer fits.

      Four eyes

      Optional: above a threshold, two people approve (off in the recorded run). On Metal, a 2-of-2 account refused one signature (test world).

      Emergency stop

      One Face ID stops every agent and freezes every card at Airwallex. (Tested on stand-ins; not yet run live.)

      Fakes and AI tricks blocked

      Changed bank details and hidden "pre-approved" lines are caught before any money moves.

      Speed limits, and our own red team

      An agent that pays too often or too much is stopped by code. Our red team attacks Purser; every attack must fail.

      A log you can check

      Every payment and approval in a hash-chained log you can re-check offline.

      Built for the rules, not certified. In production Airwallex, the regulated provider, does KYB/KYC, card issuing and sanctions screening (the sandbox demo does not exercise KYC or screening); Metal enforces the limits on chain; Purser holds no funds of its own (its runner keeps the testnet owner key that binds the mandates), logs every AI step and keeps a person in charge, in the spirit of the EU AI Act, two-factor approval and minimal personal data.
